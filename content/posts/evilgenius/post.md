# Video Game Review: Evil Genius

I had seen this game years ago, I had played the demo, which was actually fairly different, but never got around to playing the actual game. Fun fact, it was the game that introduced me to "Good old Games" though was not my first purchase there. That would be roller coster tycoon. Anyway. There is a lot to like about this game. The general idea is a base management sim with the "evil genius trying to take over the world" styling. Modeled after things like the James Bond series with man character references like the super agent "John Steele" who is clearly modeled after James Bond himself. It's a fun game with a few problems I will go into shortly.

# Good Things

## Acts of Imfamy and the World Map

The Acts of Imfamy and sending minions on missions on the world map was a great system. You could select an area on the world map and then send minions to that location to do one of three basic missions. 

1. Steal - Your main source of income
2. Plot - The way you discover acts of Imfamy
3. Hide - Hide, when there is law enforcement in the area.

These were simple options yet effective. Which is a pervailing theme of this game honestly, simple yet effective. It feels like they trimmed anything that wasn't necessary to game play to keep the game simple. There wasn't anything needed beyond this to keep the game rolling. 

The Acts of Imfamy were the extra bit that made the world map. These were specific missions that you could perform to quire notority, loot items, or various game objectives. These required that you have various minions of certain classes to accomplish and if you added extras you boosted your chances of success. They were interesting, and again, fairly simple so you didn't feel belabored trying to accomplish them.

## Loot Items

These came from Acts of Infamy and were items that would show up around your base. These provided buffs and research objects which meant you wanted to distributed them through your base. They were effectively little buff items that you could only get one of. This also kept you from just building the best one everywhere. It created variety. I liked it.

## Henchmen

Most base sim games don't give you direct control over your minions. This is good because you aren't supposed to be giving orders at that level. But there are always occations where something needs to be done immediately. In some games, like Oxygen Not Included, you can set a priority as emergency so it usurps all other tasks until it is done. In Evil Genius this job is given to your "Henchmen" which you have a set number of, starting with one. However the only task you can demand is attacking an enemy. Basic, but still useful. It is important to remember to provide some sort of system. 

## Simplified Finances

There was no perpetual drain on your resources. e.g. you didn't have to pay your minions. This was another great simplification. This meant that if you needed to you could just kind of turtle up and recover if you were in dire straights minion numbers wise. This also means it was one less detail you had to pay attention to. Your money only mattered if you were trying to build things or recruit minions. 

# Bad Things

## No Priority System

I hinted at this in the henchmen comments. There is no priority system in the game. So if there is something of significant priority to your base safety, like say building a front door, your minions will go about their business treating that objective as equally as important as anything else. It would be very nice to mark certain tasks as higher priority than others so they could get done sooner. I had a point where I had to cancel a bunch of construction that I had planned out to keep myself from running out of power because they just weren't prioritorizing the new powerplants I had requested. 

## No nogo zones

There were times that I wanted to block off certain areas to certain minions. Particularly I had problems with my social minions wandering into combat zones and getting slaughtered, it was VERY annoying. 

## Door Guarding linked to Security Level

The doors had 4 security levels, anyone, minions, evil genius, evil genius and guarded. Now, this is an instance I think of simplification creating problems. Practically speaking security level 3, and there for 4, were useless. You could put the evil genius in a safe place and deny access to anyone else, but the enemy agents would still get in and now your minions can't get there to help. Security level 4 had one use though, it was the only way to garuntee some henchmen at a particular location. I, and many other players, discovered that you could make a short dead end hallway that you put a level 4 door on and you have guards! why this was only available on a door that is effectively impassible is beyond me. This should be a toggle unrelated to security level. I found that there were times I would even appreciate a door open to everyone that is guarded. 

## Interesting research

I was quite fond of the way the research system works. The basic idea is your science minions wonder your base looking at various things searching for inspiration for research. You then select research equipment to operate on and they would do the research. It was much more interesting than a tech tree which you just select "research this next". However it was actually very frustrating. You could provide no guidance to your science minions and that results in just a lot of waiting and hoping. There was no history of previous failed research attempts so it was regularly re-doing things that you had already researched which was not enjoyable. Lastly that system provides interest only the first time through. Subsequent times or if you just got tired of spamming the research until you got it right and looked at the wiki it had no mistique any more. 

## Crowded World map

About half way through the game you past victories crowded the map and it made it really hard to see anything. There were toggles to turn various things off, but turning off your past victories specifically were not one of them. 

## No time controls

There were plenty of times where I just needed to wait for something to happen. There was no fast forward or pause. This is purely a quality of life feature and it was annoying to not have it.

# Conclusion

I enjoyed the game, but I got bored of it after my 20+ hours of play and didn't finish it. The objectives became opaque towards the end and I lost interest. There were a number of creature comforts that would have helped me keep interest, and that is overall I think the game's biggest failure. The lack of visibility into what was going on made me loose interest. Which is also something I noticed when I've tried to play even older games like Sim City 2000. Visibility matters. It gives the player a sense of agency and that makes the game fun. Several of the mechanics of Evil Genius I greatly enjoyed and plan on using in games I want to make.
