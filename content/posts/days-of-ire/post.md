# Board Game Review: Days of Ire

I received Days of Ire from a coworker and decided to give it a try. With My little 2 year old running around it is exceedingly difficult to play games with my Wife because Lucy is always demanding our attention when we look away from her to play the game. So this was an exciting option as it did not require two players to play. As a result I have claimed a table left by the previous owners of our house in our basement. A nice green matted table for playing poker. Fortunately is is purly a green square, no poker chip holders or anything like that so it works well for playing board games. 

I setup the game, and played it twice. The second time took a while to get to because the game just did not entice me. I would walk by it in my basement every evening when I came down to tend to my cat but there it sat. Finally I decided "lets go play it already." and I'm glad I did. It only took me about an hour. I technically won, but only after some fudging of the rules. Looking at the stack of "event" cards that were left unplayed, something that shouldn't have happened, i probably didn't actually win. So there is definately an aspect of me failing that leads to my less than stellar opinion of it. But I think the other thing is it's kind of a "calamity" game, where you are attempting to just forstall the end rather than build up to something. I tend to like games involving building something up. It would probably have been more fun with more people, with only one person I felt like there was not enough that I could do to make a difference. But those details aside, lets talk about things I've learned.

## Active Fighters

The game had this concept of "Active fighers" which were geo-restricted buffs or "items" depending on the fighter. This was an interesting idea as they were far more dependable than the cards in your hand and were never depleted. This took a little bit of emphasis off the player's hand. I was limited to only carrying two with me at a time though, this mean that I only had so many with me, which made them a bit hard to use. If I had been 4 players instead of 1 perhaps that would have been more powerful. In fact I feel fairly certainly so as the game does not scale for players in nearly any aspect. e.g. every round 4 cards were given to 4 revolutionary players, or 4 card were given to one. Similarly only 4 actions could be taken per turn, whether there was one player or four. But 4 players could be geographically disperate much easier than 1 player. And each turn all 4 players get one free movement, while the one player only gets one free movement. 

## Communication

This one I'm theorizing about. The rules state that the revolutionary players are not supposed to give specifics to each other about what is in their hand. I feel like one of the big selling points on this game would have been the communication and coordination of 4 players to attempt to win a revolution. But since I played by myself, I didn't get that. And unlike other games, such as catan, I can't actually play 4 players at once and get this same effect. 

Overall one of the things I noticed is the game didn't excite me enough to really put my noggin joggin as I clearly would need to be doing in order to beat the game. 

## Abstraction: A game without metaphor

Days of Ire is of course a metaphor for the hungarian revolution. All games about historical events are. It got me thinking about how it would be interesting to distill this game down to it's pure abstract form. Instead of "ammo", "Supplies", "news", "medicine", and "transportation" we have say "squares", "circles", "triangles", "pentagons", and "hexagon the bestagon". Instead of "inguries" we just have points that can't reach 4 or 5. The infantry, snipes, and tanks were already triangles, squares, and hexagons respectively so those are easy. And so on and so forth until the game is just a plain white board with a network of number "locations" upon which your shapes "battle". What would that game look like? How would it inform you opinion on how to make the game better? 

I feel like when a game designer sets about creating a game they start first with the idea of "here is my setting" and ask "how do I make this a game?" Mechanics and the dynamics of a game are inspired by the setting and the sort of feeling you want to aim for. I may try one of these days stripping a game down to its barebones like that and seeing how it changes my perspective on it.
